CHAPTER 1: BUSINESS WRITING


Proposals & Pitches


P1 — Client Proposal

"Write a professional freelance proposal for a client who needs [describe the project]. My business is [name/type]. The proposal should include: project understanding, my approach, deliverables, timeline, pricing (Rs[amount]), and a closing paragraph. Keep it under 400 words. Professional but warm tone."


P2 — Investor Pitch (1-Pager)

"Write a 1-page investor pitch for my business: [describe your business in 2-3 sentences]. Include: the problem we solve, our solution, market size, business model, traction so far ([mention any revenue/users/milestones]), and what we're asking for ([Rs X for X% equity or loan]). Clear, confident, concise."


P3 — Partnership Proposal

"Write an email proposing a business partnership between [My Company] and [Their Company]. We want to collaborate on [describe the collaboration]. The benefit for them: [describe]. The benefit for us: [describe]. Keep the email under 300 words and end with a specific ask (e.g., 30-min call this week)."


P4 — Service Quotation Email

"Write a professional quotation email for a client who asked about [service]. Itemise the cost: [list items and prices]. Include: what's covered, what's not, payment terms ([50% upfront, balance on delivery etc.]), and validity of the quote ([15 days]). Tone: professional but friendly."


P5 — Annual Report Summary (for small business)

"Summarise this year's business performance for [Business Name] in a clear, professional paragraph for our annual report / stakeholder update. Key numbers: Revenue: Rs[X]. Expenses: Rs[X]. Profit: Rs[X]. Customer count: [X]. Top achievement: [describe]. Challenge faced: [describe]. Goal for next year: [describe]."



Contracts & Legal Language


P6 — Freelance Contract Clause

"Write a professional contract clause for: [describe what you want to cover, e.g., 'client revisions are limited to 2 rounds', or 'payment is due within 7 days of invoice']. Language should be clear and legally reasonable but not overly intimidating. Suitable for a freelancer in India."


P7 — NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) Summary

"Write a simple, plain-language NDA summary for a freelance project between me ([Your Name/Business]) and [Client Name]. Key points to cover: confidentiality of project details, no sharing of client data, duration ([6 months / 1 year]), what happens on breach. Simple enough that both parties clearly understand it."


P8 — Terms and Conditions (for a service business)

"Write Terms and Conditions for [Business Name], a [type of business] in India. Cover: payment terms, refund policy ([describe your policy]), service scope, revision policy, cancellation policy, and dispute resolution. Make it professional but readable — not full of legal jargon."



Invoices & Finance


P9 — Invoice Description

"Write a professional invoice description for the following work: [describe what you delivered]. The description should be clear, specific, and make the deliverable sound valuable. 2-3 sentences max."


P10 — Payment Reminder Email (Friendly)

"Write a friendly payment reminder email to a client whose invoice of Rs[amount] (Invoice #[number], dated [date]) is now [X days] overdue. Tone: polite, professional, not aggressive. Remind them of the invoice, ask if there are any issues, and provide UPI details: [your UPI ID]."


P11 — Payment Reminder Email (Firm)

"Write a firm but professional payment reminder email. The invoice of Rs[amount] is [X days] overdue despite a friendly reminder sent on [date]. This email should communicate urgency and the next step (e.g., work will pause if not paid by [date]). Professional, not rude."


P12 — GST Summary Explanation

"Explain this GST summary in simple language for a small business owner who is not a finance expert: [paste your GST figures or describe]. Include: what CGST/SGST/IGST means, what I owe, what I can claim as input credit, and any red flags I should be aware of."



CHAPTER 2: MARKETING & SOCIAL MEDIA


Instagram


P13 — Instagram Caption (Educational)

"Write an Instagram caption for [Business Name] ([describe what you do]). Topic: [specific topic or tip you want to share]. Target audience: [describe]. Tone: [casual/professional/inspirational]. Length: 150-200 words. End with a question to drive comments and 3 lines of relevant hashtags."


P14 — Instagram Caption (Product Launch)

"Write an Instagram caption announcing the launch of [Product/Service Name]. Price: Rs[X]. Key benefit: [describe]. Who it's for: [describe]. Include urgency (limited slots/stock). End with a clear CTA. Tone: excited but not spammy. 100-150 words."


P15 — Instagram Bio

"Write 3 versions of an Instagram bio for [Business Name]. We [describe what you do]. Target audience: [describe]. Tone options: (1) Professional (2) Casual/Friendly (3) Bold/Punchy. Each version must be under 150 characters and include a CTA."


P16 — Instagram Story Sequence

"Write a 5-slide Instagram Story sequence to promote [product/service]. Slide 1: Hook (a question or bold statement). Slide 2: Problem. Slide 3: Solution. Slide 4: Proof/testimonial. Slide 5: CTA. Each slide: max 15 words. Bold, punchy, action-oriented."


P17 — Instagram Reel Script

"Write a 30-second Instagram Reel script for [Business Type]. Topic: [specific tip or insight]. Format: talking head or voiceover. Hook (first 3 seconds): [grab attention immediately with a bold statement or question]. Body: [3 quick tips or one insight]. CTA: [what to do next]. Punchy, fast-paced, no filler words."



WhatsApp & Telegram


P18 — WhatsApp Broadcast Message

"Write a WhatsApp broadcast message for [Business Name] announcing [offer/update/event]. Audience: existing customers. Length: under 150 words. Include: what's new, why they should care, and a simple next step (DM, call, or link). Conversational tone — like a message from a friend, not a press release."


P19 — WhatsApp Follow-Up After Meeting

"Write a WhatsApp follow-up message to send after a business meeting/discovery call with [type of prospect]. Key points discussed: [brief notes]. Next step: [what I promised to send or do]. Keep it short, warm, professional. Under 100 words."


P20 — Telegram Channel Announcement

"Write a Telegram channel post announcing [news/product/event]. Format: short headline, 3-4 bullet points with the key information, and a CTA. Telegram readers scroll fast — make it scannable. Under 200 words."



Email Marketing


P21 — Welcome Email (New Subscriber)

"Write a welcome email for new subscribers of [Business Name]'s email list. What they signed up for: [describe the lead magnet or reason they subscribed]. Tone: warm, personal, like you're talking to one person. Include: who you are, what to expect from future emails, and one immediate value (tip, resource, or free thing). Under 250 words."


P22 — Email Newsletter (Weekly)

"Write a weekly email newsletter for [Business Name] — [describe your niche]. This week's topic: [topic]. Format: short intro (2-3 sentences), main content (tip/insight/story — 150-200 words), and a brief CTA at the end. Tone: [conversational/expert/inspirational]. Subject line options: give me 5."


P23 — Re-engagement Email (Cold Leads)

"Write an email to re-engage cold leads who haven't opened our emails in [X months]. Business: [describe]. What we want them to do: [describe CTA]. Make it self-aware — acknowledge they haven't heard from us in a while. Offer something of value to bring them back. Under 200 words. 3 subject line options."


P24 — Cart Abandonment Email

"Write a cart abandonment email for [Business/Product Name]. The customer added [Product] to their cart but didn't buy. Price: Rs[X]. Key benefit: [describe]. Include a gentle reminder, address a possible objection (e.g., 'not sure if it's right for you?'), and add urgency (limited stock / offer ending). Under 150 words."


P25 — Product Launch Email Sequence (3 emails)

"Write a 3-email launch sequence for [Product/Service]. Email 1 (3 days before): teaser — build curiosity. Email 2 (launch day): full announcement with benefits, price, and CTA. Email 3 (2 days after): last chance — urgency + address top objection. Each email: under 200 words. Subject lines included."



CHAPTER 3: HR & TEAM MANAGEMENT


P26 — Job Description

"Write a job description for a [role] at [Company Name]. We are a [describe your company]. The role involves: [3-4 key responsibilities]. Requirements: [skills, experience, qualifications]. What we offer: [salary, benefits, culture description]. Tone: professional but human — we want good people, not just qualified ones."


P27 — Offer Letter

"Write an offer letter for [Candidate Name] for the role of [Position] at [Company Name]. Start date: [date]. Salary: Rs[amount] per month. Probation: [X months]. Reporting to: [Name/role]. Include standard clauses: confidentiality, IP ownership, notice period ([X days]). Professional Indian business English."


P28 — Appraisal Feedback

"Write constructive performance appraisal feedback for an employee in the [role] role. Strengths observed: [list 2-3]. Areas for improvement: [list 2-3]. Goal for next 6 months: [describe]. Tone: honest, encouraging, growth-focused. Not harsh, not falsely positive. 200-250 words."


P29 — Warning Letter

"Write a professional warning letter to an employee ([Name], [role]) regarding [describe the issue: e.g., repeated tardiness, misconduct, underperformance]. Tone: firm but fair. Include: the specific behaviour, how it violates policy, expected change, consequence if it continues, and a note that we want to support their improvement. Professional Indian business tone."


P30 — Team Meeting Agenda

"Write a structured meeting agenda for a [monthly/weekly/quarterly] team meeting for [Business Type]. Duration: [X minutes]. Topics to cover: [list your topics]. Include time allocation for each item and a 5-minute slot for open questions/comments. Clear, concise format."



CHAPTER 4: CUSTOMER SERVICE


P31 — Complaint Response (Polite)

"Write a response to an unhappy customer who complained about [describe the issue]. We want to: acknowledge the problem, apologise sincerely, explain what went wrong (briefly), and offer a resolution: [describe what you'll do to fix it]. Tone: empathetic, not defensive. Under 150 words."


P32 — Refund Request Response

"Write a response to a customer requesting a refund for [product/service]. Our refund policy: [describe it]. We [will / will not] issue a full refund because [reason]. If we can't refund, offer an alternative: [store credit / replacement / partial refund]. Tone: fair, kind, professional."


P33 — FAQ Page Content

"Write an FAQ section for [Business Name] ([describe what you do]). Include 8-10 questions customers typically ask about: pricing, delivery/turnaround, revisions, guarantees, payment methods, and getting started. Answers should be concise (2-4 sentences each) and in a friendly, helpful tone."



CHAPTER 5: CONTENT CREATION


P34 — Blog Article Outline

"Create a detailed outline for a 1000-word SEO blog article targeting the keyword: '[keyword]'. Include: recommended H1 title, 5-7 H2 sections with brief bullet points of what to cover in each, and a suggested conclusion CTA. The article should target [Indian audience / beginners / business owners etc.]."


P35 — Full Blog Article

"Write a complete 1000-word SEO blog article for the keyword: '[keyword]'. Target audience: [describe]. Tone: [informative/conversational]. Structure: introduction, [5-6 sections with H2 headers], conclusion with a CTA linking to [your product/service]. The article should be genuinely useful — not keyword-stuffed fluff."


P36 — LinkedIn Article

"Write a 600-word LinkedIn article for [Your Name], [role/title at Company]. Topic: [specific topic]. Audience: [Indian professionals/entrepreneurs/job seekers]. Tone: expert with a personal point of view — share an opinion, not just facts. End with a discussion question to drive comments."


P37 — YouTube Video Script

"Write a 5-minute YouTube video script for [Channel Name/Topic]. Video title: [title]. Structure: Hook (first 30 seconds to grab attention), Introduction (who you are, what this video covers), Main content (3-5 key points), Summary, CTA (subscribe/link in description). Conversational tone, like you're talking to one person."


P38 — Press Release

"Write a press release for [Business Name] announcing [news: e.g., product launch, funding, milestone, new partnership]. Include: headline, dateline ([City, Date]), 3-4 paragraphs (what, why it matters, quote from founder, background about the company), and boilerplate about the company. Professional PR format."



CHAPTER 6: FINANCE & GST (INDIA-SPECIFIC)


P39 — P&L Explanation

"Explain this Profit & Loss statement in plain English for a non-finance business owner. P&L data: [paste or describe your figures]. What I want to understand: Is the business profitable? Where is money being wasted? What should I focus on to improve margins next quarter?"


P40 — Cash Flow Issue Diagnosis

"My business has the following cash flow situation: [describe: revenue is there but I'm always short of cash / I have lots of receivables / my expenses are front-loaded etc.]. What are the likely causes and what are 3-5 practical steps to fix this? Keep the answer practical, not theoretical."


P41 — GST Filing Checklist

"Create a monthly GST filing checklist for a small business in India with turnover under Rs1.5 crore. Include: documents to gather, GSTR forms to file, deadlines, common mistakes to avoid, and what happens if you miss a deadline. Simple, actionable, easy to follow."


P42 — Business Plan Financial Section

"Write the financial projections section of a business plan for [Business Name] ([describe the business]). 3-year revenue forecast based on: Year 1 assumptions ([describe: e.g., 10 clients at Rs5000/month]), Year 2 growth ([e.g., 25% increase]), Year 3 growth ([e.g., 30%]). Include revenue, estimated costs, and net profit for each year. Clear table format."



CHAPTER 7: SALES & NEGOTIATION


P43 — Sales Call Script

"Write a sales call script for [Business Name] selling [product/service] to [target customer]. The call structure: warm opening, qualifying questions to understand their need, pitch (tailored to common pain points), handling objections ([list 2-3 common ones]), and closing ([specific ask]). Natural, conversational, not robotic."


P44 — Handling Price Objection

"Write a script for handling the objection: 'Your price is too high / I found someone cheaper.' My service/product: [describe]. My price: Rs[X]. Cheaper alternative they mentioned: [describe if known]. Respond without being defensive — focus on value, outcomes, and risk of choosing the cheaper option. 150 words max."


P45 — Follow-Up After Sales Call

"Write a follow-up email after a sales call with [prospect type]. We discussed: [brief notes]. They seemed interested in [specific thing]. Their main concern was [objection/hesitation]. Next step: [what I said would happen]. Remind them of the key benefit relevant to them and make the next step easy. Under 200 words."


P46 — Upsell Message

"Write a WhatsApp/email message to an existing customer to upsell them from [current plan/product] to [higher plan/product]. Key benefit of upgrading: [describe]. Price difference: Rs[X] more per month. Make it feel like a natural, helpful recommendation — not a pushy sales pitch. Under 150 words."


P47 — Asking for a Referral

"Write a message (WhatsApp or email) asking a happy customer for a referral. They purchased [product/service] and gave positive feedback. I want them to refer others without feeling awkward. Include: thank them, mention a referral incentive ([describe if any]), and make the ask specific and easy. Under 120 words."



CHAPTER 8: HINGLISH PROMPTS (For Indian Social Media)


These prompts produce content in Hinglish — the natural mix of Hindi and English used on Indian social media. Perfect for WhatsApp status, Instagram Reels captions, and Telegram posts targeting Tier 2/3 India.


H1 — Motivational Hinglish caption

"Write an Instagram caption in Hinglish (mix of Hindi and English, using English script throughout — no Devanagari). Topic: motivation for young entrepreneurs. Tone: relatable, real, like a friend talking. 80-100 words. End with a relatable question."


H2 — Product promotion in Hinglish

"Write a promotional WhatsApp message in Hinglish for [Product/Service]. Offer: [describe]. Audience: young Indians aged 18-30. Tone: energetic, like a cool brand, not corporate. Under 100 words. Include a clear CTA."


H3 — Hinglish Reel voiceover

"Write a 20-second Reel voiceover script in Hinglish about [topic]. First line must be a strong hook. Fast-paced, punchy, with a relatable moment in the middle. End with a CTA. No Devanagari — English script only."


H4 — Hinglish complaint response

"Write a response to this customer complaint (in Hinglish, English script): [paste complaint]. Tone: understanding, helpful, not defensive. Under 80 words."


H5 — Festival greetings with CTA

"Write a [Diwali/Holi/Eid/New Year] greeting message in Hinglish for [Business Name]'s WhatsApp broadcast. 2-3 warm sentences of greeting, then transition to a soft promotional message about [offer]. Natural, not salesy. Under 100 words."



CHAPTER 9: AI PRODUCTIVITY HACKS


P48 — Summarise a long document

"Summarise this [document/article/contract/report] in bullet points. Focus on: key decisions or actions required, important numbers or dates, anything I need to follow up on, and any red flags. [Paste the document]"


P49 — Rewrite for different audiences

"Rewrite this content for a [15-year-old / corporate executive / rural Indian small business owner]: [paste content]. Keep the core message but adjust: vocabulary, examples, tone, and length as appropriate for the new audience."


P50 — Create a weekly schedule

"Create a productive weekly schedule for a [freelancer/solopreneur/startup founder] in India. I work [X hours/day]. My main tasks are: [list]. I need to include time for: client work, business development, admin, learning, and rest. Monday to Saturday schedule. Practical and realistic."
